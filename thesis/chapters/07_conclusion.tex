\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}
% ============================================================

This chapter synthesises the empirical findings reported in \autoref{ch:results}, maps them explicitly to the three research questions introduced in \autoref{ch:introduction}, and reflects on the contributions, limitations, and future directions of the unified forecasting-and-pooling framework developed in this thesis.

\section{Answers to Research Questions}

\subsection{RQ1: Ensemble Benchmarking}

Research Question~1 asked which of fourteen forecasting models achieves the strongest accuracy under strictly chronological evaluation, and whether rank differences are statistically significant. The evidence from \autoref{tab:results_full} and the Friedman test in \autoref{sec:significance} provides a clear answer: ensemble strategies dominate individual estimators on every reported metric, and the differences among models are not attributable to sampling variation ($\chi^2_F = 57.30$, $p < 0.001$).

Among individual models, XGBoost~\parencite{chen2016xgboost} attains the lowest RMSE of 4.4974, confirming that gradient-boosted trees capture non-linear demand interactions more effectively than linear baselines or unpruned decision trees. Among ensemble strategies, Stacking~\parencite{wolpert1992stacking} achieves the best overall performance on RMSE (4.3008), and R\textsuperscript{2} (0.9922), while Blending leads on MAPE (9.30\%), and RMSLE (0.1226). Simple Averaging, Weighted Averaging, and the Voting Regressor produce near-identical results, reflecting their shared base-estimator set and confirming that performance-weighted combination offers marginal benefit over equal weighting when base models are already well calibrated.

The pairwise Wilcoxon post-hoc tests, corrected for multiple comparisons, do not reach the Bonferroni threshold with only five folds (a known power limitation rather than evidence of equivalence), but the Friedman rejection establishes that practitioners cannot treat the fourteen models as interchangeable. For operational deployment, the choice between Stacking and Blending therefore reduces to the metric that best aligns with business objectives: absolute dispatch error (RMSE, R\textsuperscript{2}) favours Stacking; proportional or log-scale error (MAPE, RMSLE) favours Blending.

\subsection{RQ2: Hybrid Residual Modelling}

Research Question~2 asked whether a two-stage LR-LSTM hybrid improves spatio-temporal demand prediction relative to standalone baselines, and how an XGBoost-LSTM variant compares when a stronger non-linear first stage replaces linear regression. The LR-LSTM Hybrid~\parencite{kim2020hybrid} achieves the second-best MAPE among individual models (11.64\%), and the best RMSLE (0.1443), demonstrating that dedicated LSTM residual modelling captures intra-day non-linear dynamics that linear regression alone cannot represent. Its RMSE of 6.0529, however, exceeds that of XGBoost and all ensemble methods, indicating that the linear first stage leaves spatial interaction effects underfit when demand variance is high.

The XGBoost-LSTM variant partially addresses this limitation: substituting XGBoost for linear regression as the first stage reduces RMSE from 6.0529 to 4.9658, yet the hybrid still trails Stacking and Blending. This pattern suggests that residual decomposition is a principled architectural choice (separating predictable linear structure from sequential dynamics), but that the greatest gains come from combining diverse model families rather than refining any single hybrid pipeline. Relative to the Kim et al.~\parencite{kim2020hybrid} benchmark (R\textsuperscript{2}~=~0.572, MAPE~=~15.2\%), the present LR-LSTM configuration achieves R\textsuperscript{2}~=~0.9846 and MAPE~=~11.64\%, a substantial improvement attributable to the richer feature set, the $K{=}40$ cluster granularity, and the chronological evaluation protocol rather than to architectural novelty alone.

\subsection{RQ3: Constraint-Aware Ride Pooling}

Research Question~3 asked what pool rate, per-trip savings, and city-scale impact a multi-criteria greedy matcher can achieve under realistic passenger-acceptance constraints, and how these outcomes compare with theoretical bounds and naive baselines. Algorithm~\ref{alg:ride_pool} achieves a pool rate of 16.06\% on 106,055 sampled trips, with a mean compatibility score of 0.658 and mean per-trip saving of 19.74\%, validated by 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals over 10,000 iterations. The operational rate lies well below the $\sim$40\% trip-length reduction bound of~\parencite{santi2014quantifying}, but this gap is expected: the shareability network assumes unconstrained matching, whereas the present algorithm enforces eight-minute temporal windows, 0.75-mile origin and destination proximity, 45-degree directional alignment, and a 27\% maximum detour ratio informed by~\parencite{furuhata2013ridesharing} and~\parencite{tang2018joint}.

Comparison with naive baselines in \autoref{tab:pooling_baseline} confirms that maximising pool rate alone is counterproductive: random pairing achieves 100\% pool rate but only 5.2\% per-trip savings, while temporal-only matching yields 68.4\% pool rate with compatibility scores of 0.312. The proposed multi-criteria algorithm trades raw volume for match quality, producing the highest per-trip savings and compatibility scores across all three strategies. City-scale projection yields annual cost savings of \$28.7~million, 8.20~million miles avoided, 3,370~tonnes of CO\textsubscript{2} reduced, and 547,000 passenger-hours saved when scaled to the verified 2015 NYC annual volume~\parencite{nyc_opendata_2015}. The poolability classification experiment (\autoref{sec:pool_eval}) further establishes that trip-level features alone cannot predict poolability at booking time, implying that reactive pairwise matching rather than proactive single-trip filtering is the appropriate operational architecture.

\section{Summary of Contributions}

This research introduces a unified data-driven framework for shared urban taxi systems, integrating ensemble-based demand forecasting with multi-criteria ride pooling within a single statistically validated pipeline. The contributions span three domains that prior literature has typically addressed in isolation.

First, the thesis delivers a systematic ensemble benchmarking study of fourteen forecasting models on identical NYC Yellow Taxi cluster-bin targets under strictly chronological splits. By comparing eight individual estimators and six ensemble strategies with Friedman and Wilcoxon testing, the study establishes that ensemble combination (not architectural novelty in any single model) is the most reliable path to closing the residual prediction gap. Stacking achieves R\textsuperscript{2}~=~0.9922, representing a 73.5\% improvement over the Kim et al.~\parencite{kim2020hybrid} LR-LSTM benchmark on the same dataset family.

Second, the thesis proposes and evaluates a two-stage LR-LSTM hybrid architecture alongside an XGBoost-LSTM variant, extending the Kim et al.~\parencite{kim2020hybrid} residual-decomposition design with a richer feature matrix, validated cluster granularity, and controlled comparison against gradient-boosted and ensemble baselines. The hybrid variants confirm that sequential residual modelling adds value for percentage-error metrics, but that ensemble meta-learning supersedes any single hybrid pipeline on aggregate squared error.

Third, the thesis develops a constraint-aware ride pooling algorithm that instantiates the Furuhata et al.~\parencite{furuhata2013ridesharing} matching criteria as hard filters and quantifies operational returns with bootstrap confidence intervals and city-scale impact projection. The algorithm is deployable in real time: greedy batch matching operates in $O(B^2)$ per 5-minute window with $B \leq 20$, consistent with the tractability analysis of~\parencite{agatz2012ridesharing}. The negative poolability classification result is itself a contribution, demonstrating that shared-taxi systems must match reactively on concurrent trip streams rather than filter proactively on individual bookings.

By evaluating forecasting and pooling within a single reproducible pipeline on the same NYC Yellow Taxi dataset, the thesis enables practitioners to assess both predictive accuracy and operational consolidation returns under unified assumptions, a combination absent from the prior work summarised in \autoref{tab:litreview}.

\section{Key Findings}

Several findings recur across the experimental programme and merit explicit restatement for practitioners and researchers.

Ensemble strategies consistently outperform individual models. All six ensemble methods surpass every individual estimator on RMSE and R\textsuperscript{2}, with Stacking recording the lowest RMSE (4.3008), and Blending the lowest MAPE (9.30\%). The box-plot analysis in \autoref{fig:significance} confirms that ensemble methods also exhibit narrower fold-to-fold variance, indicating genuine robustness rather than test-set overfitting.

Different ensemble architectures excel on different metrics. Stacking minimises aggregate squared error through out-of-fold cross-validation meta-learning; Blending's holdout-based construction appears to regularise predictions in low-demand cluster-time-bins where proportional errors dominate. Operators should select the ensemble strategy that aligns with their dispatch objective function rather than assuming a single ``best'' model.

Linear models with engineered lag and cyclical features already achieve $R^2 > 0.98$, indicating that much predictable variance is captured by temporal autocorrelation and spatial centroids alone. The remaining challenge (and the primary value of ensemble and hybrid methods) is modelling the non-linear residual structure that separates good forecasts from excellent ones during peak-demand regimes.

Quality-constrained pooling outperforms volume-maximising strategies. The 16.06\% pool rate paired with 19.74\% per-trip savings demonstrates that constraint-aware matching is the appropriate optimisation target for real-world deployment, in contrast to theoretical shareability bounds~\parencite{santi2014quantifying} or fleet-level assignment frameworks~\parencite{alonso2017demand} that do not report pairwise economic quantification under passenger-acceptance limits.

Non-parametric significance testing is both tractable and necessary. The Friedman test rejects the null of identical model performance ($p < 0.001$), establishing that reported accuracy rankings are statistically meaningful even when Bonferroni-corrected pairwise tests lack power with five folds. This protocol should be adopted as standard practice in spatio-temporal forecasting benchmarks.

\section{Limitations}

Several limitations bound the present findings and should inform interpretation of the reported results.

Weather features are available only at daily resolution from NOAA, limiting sensitivity to intra-day meteorological shocks such as sudden storms or temperature drops that affect demand within a single 10-minute bin. A finer-grained hourly or sub-hourly weather feed would be expected to yield modest but measurable accuracy improvements, particularly for outer-borough clusters where weather-driven mode shift is more pronounced.

Ride pooling is evaluated on a stratified sample of 106,055 trips rather than the full 34-million-trip test corpus. Bootstrap confidence intervals confirm stability within the sample, but full-population analysis may reveal small distributional differences in pool rate and per-trip savings. The algorithm matches rides in pairs only; extending to three- or four-rider pools would require combinatorial matching with substantially increased computational complexity and different passenger-acceptance dynamics.

Willingness-to-share is assumed once all five compatibility filters are satisfied. In practice, passenger acceptance varies with demographics, trip purpose, and time of day, and is not captured by the available trip records. Detour estimates use average in-city travel speeds rather than live congestion, road closures, or turn restrictions; actual detour lengths in operational traffic may differ from the static estimates used here.

The chronological evaluation spans an 11-month train-test gap that tests seasonal generalisation but does not capture the structural demand shifts observed during the COVID-19 period documented by~\parencite{liu2021epidemic}. Graph-based forecasters such as DCRNN~\parencite{li2018diffusion} and STAEformer~\parencite{liu2023staeformer} require road-topology data unavailable in the NYC Yellow Taxi Trip Records and therefore lie outside the experimental scope.

With five chronological cross-validation folds, Bonferroni-corrected pairwise Wilcoxon tests have limited statistical power; none of the individual Stacking comparisons reach the corrected threshold of $0.0038$. The Friedman test remains the primary significance evidence. City-scale impact projections assume a constant pool rate when scaled linearly from the sample to the full annual volume; sustained operational deployment at full scale may encounter saturation effects not captured by the present analysis.

\section{Future Work}

Future research can extend the present framework along several directions suggested by the limitations above and by emerging methods in the literature.

Incorporating finer-grained weather feeds, hourly precipitation, real-time temperature, or radar-derived storm tracks, would improve demand sensitivity to intra-day shocks and may partially address the poolability prediction challenge by enriching the feature set available at booking time. Full-population pooling analysis across all 34 million test trips would strengthen confidence in the reported pool rate and savings estimates.

Multi-passenger matching beyond pairwise consolidation represents a natural algorithmic extension. The shareability network analysis of~\parencite{santi2014quantifying} suggests that larger groups can achieve higher cumulative savings, but combinatorial complexity and passenger-acceptance constraints increase correspondingly. Behavioural willingness-to-share models, informed by stated-preference surveys or revealed-preference data from operational ride-hailing platforms, would replace the implicit acceptance assumption embedded in the current five-filter design.

Graph-based demand forecasters such as DCRNN~\parencite{li2018diffusion} or STAEformer-style architectures~\parencite{liu2023staeformer} could replace the linear first stage of the hybrid model where road topology is available, potentially capturing inter-zone spatial dependencies that cluster centroids alone cannot encode. The XGBoost-LSTM variant explored here partially addresses this gap but does not model explicit zone adjacency.

Larger fold counts, ten or more chronological cross-validation folds, would strengthen pairwise post-hoc testing and allow Bonferroni-corrected Wilcoxon comparisons to reach significance reliably. Rolling-window retraining protocols would evaluate model stability under continuous deployment rather than the single train-test split adopted here.

The non-parametric significance protocol introduced in this thesis challenges the convention of reporting single-run accuracy figures without cross-model validation. Adopting Friedman testing as standard practice in spatio-temporal forecasting would improve the credibility of comparative studies and reduce the risk that reported gains reflect favourable test partitions rather than genuine architectural superiority.

\section{Closing Remarks}

Shared urban taxi systems face a dual challenge: operators must anticipate demand at neighbourhood granularity to deploy vehicles efficiently, and they must consolidate compatible trips to reduce per-passenger cost and environmental impact. Prior research has pursued these capabilities separately (demand forecasting without operational pooling validation, or pooling optimisation without accompanying demand intelligence) leaving practitioners without an integrated evidence base for system design.

This thesis demonstrates that principled co-design can address both challenges within a single reproducible pipeline. Ensemble meta-learning achieves state-of-the-art forecasting accuracy with statistical validation; constraint-aware greedy matching produces operationally realistic shared rides with bootstrap-validated economic and environmental returns. The negative poolability result is as informative as the positive forecasting and pooling outcomes: it establishes that shared-taxi matching must operate reactively on concurrent trip streams, not proactively on individual bookings.

The framework is intentionally modular. Each pipeline stage (spatial clustering, temporal binning, feature engineering, model training, ensemble combination, pooling matching, and impact projection) is implemented as an independent notebook, enabling operators to substitute components, adjust constraint thresholds, or retrain models on rolling windows without redesigning the entire system. For a city the scale of New York, even the conservative 16.06\% pool rate projected here represents measurable economic and environmental benefit, and the forecasting subsystem provides the dispatch intelligence necessary to realise that benefit at operational scale.
