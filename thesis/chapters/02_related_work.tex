\chapter{Related Work}
\label{ch:related}
% ============================================================

Prior research on urban taxi systems can be organised across three threads: demand forecasting, ride pooling, and ensemble methods. This chapter reviews each thread in turn, critically assessing what each line of work achieves and where it falls short for the joint forecasting-and-pooling problem addressed in this thesis. A positioning section concludes by mapping these gaps to the contributions of the present study.

\section{Demand Forecasting}

Early demand forecasting methods relied on ARIMA variants, which capture linear temporal patterns but cannot encode spatial correlations across neighbourhood zones. Dia et al.~\parencite{dia2002object} established early data-driven baselines for object-oriented demand prediction, demonstrating that historical trip patterns contain exploitable structure, but their methods predate the deep learning and ensemble advances that define the current state of the art. Moreira-Matias et al.~\parencite{moreira2013predicting} demonstrated streaming-based demand prediction from real-time GPS data, establishing that short-horizon forecasts are feasible at operational latency, but their evaluation did not incorporate spatial clustering or ensemble combination. Lv et al.~\parencite{lv2015traffic} showed that deep learning outperforms time-series baselines on large-scale traffic flow data, yet their grid-based representation differs materially from the cluster-bin pickup counts used here. Zhang et al.~\parencite{zhang2017deep} introduced ST-ResNet, which treats citywide demand grids as image sequences processed by deep residual convolutions; while visually compelling, grid methods require careful alignment of cell boundaries with actual demand hotspots and report crowd-flow metrics that are not directly comparable to per-cluster pickup counts.

Xu et al.~\parencite{xu2017realtime} confirmed that LSTM networks outperform feed-forward architectures for real-time NYC taxi demand by exploiting sequential autocorrelation, achieving MAPE~$\approx$12.5\%. This result motivates the LSTM component of the LR-LSTM hybrid in the present study, but Xu et al.\ did not decompose demand into linear trend and non-linear residual components, nor did they benchmark against gradient-boosted tree ensembles. Ma et al.~\parencite{ma2015long} and Ke et al.~\parencite{ke2017short} further validated LSTM superiority for transportation speed and ride-hailing demand tasks respectively, reinforcing the value of recurrent architectures for mobility time series while leaving open the question of whether a simpler linear first stage can capture much of the predictable variance.

Kim et al.~\parencite{kim2020hybrid} proposed the LR-LSTM model, a two-stage hybrid achieving R\textsuperscript{2}~=~0.572 and MAPE~=~15.2\% on Murray Hill, NYC, which serves as the primary benchmark in this study. The Kim et al.\ design demonstrates that residual decomposition is a principled strategy, but their evaluation covers a single neighbourhood zone, uses a single hybrid architecture, and reports no statistical significance testing across competing models. Liu et al.~\parencite{liu2021epidemic} reported XGBoost attaining R\textsuperscript{2}~=~0.984 on NYC Yellow Taxi data, highlighting distributional shift as a key challenge during the COVID-19 period; their result confirms that gradient-boosted trees are strong individual predictors, but the study period and feature set differ from the pre-pandemic chronological split adopted here. Guo et al.~\parencite{guo2019attention} proposed attention-based spatio-temporal graph convolutional networks, demonstrating that graph structures capture inter-zone dependencies more effectively than grid-based methods when adjacency is well defined.

Li et al.~\parencite{li2018diffusion} introduced DCRNN, which encodes directional diffusion on road graphs; this approach is theoretically attractive for capturing traffic propagation, but it requires detailed road-topology data unavailable in the NYC Yellow Taxi Trip Records used in the present study. Zhou et al.~\parencite{zhou2018predicting} applied attention mechanisms to multi-step citywide passenger demand prediction, and Yao et al.~\parencite{yao2018deeptraffic} proposed DeepMVST for multi-view spatio-temporal forecasting on NYC taxi data. More recently, Liu et al.~\parencite{liu2023staeformer} proposed STAEformer, which augments a vanilla Transformer with spatio-temporal adaptive embeddings and achieves state-of-the-art results on standard traffic speed and flow benchmarks including METR-LA and PEMS-BAY. Because STAEformer targets road-network speed prediction rather than zone-level pickup-count forecasting, direct numerical comparison with the present study is not feasible, but it represents the current frontier of spatio-temporal deep learning and motivates future work on graph-based demand forecasting.

\paragraph{Critical assessment for this problem.} The demand forecasting literature establishes three durable lessons relevant to RQ1 and RQ2: gradient-boosted trees and LSTM networks are individually strong; hybrid decomposition can separate predictable linear structure from residual dynamics; and deep graph models require infrastructure data absent from standard taxi trip records. What the literature collectively fails to provide is a controlled comparison of fourteen models on identical cluster-bin targets with non-parametric significance testing, a gap this thesis closes in \autoref{ch:results}. For the specific task of predicting pickup counts in 40 data-driven clusters at 10-minute resolution, linear models with engineered lag and cyclical features already achieve $R^2 > 0.98$, suggesting that much of the predictable variance is captured by temporal autocorrelation and spatial centroids alone; the remaining challenge is modelling the non-linear residual structure that separates a good forecast from an excellent one. Ensemble combination, rather than architectural novelty in any single model, emerges as the most reliable path to closing that gap.

\section{Ride Pooling}

On the ride pooling side, Santi et al.~\parencite{santi2014quantifying} established via shareability network analysis that ride consolidation can achieve up to $\sim$40\% cumulative trip-length reduction across the NYC fleet under idealised conditions, providing a theoretical upper bound for any operational algorithm. This bound is invaluable as a ceiling reference, but the shareability network assumes passengers accept any compatible match without detour or waiting constraints, conditions that no real-world operator can guarantee. Alonso-Mora et al.~\parencite{alonso2017demand} developed a real-time framework serving nearly the entire NYC demand with 15\% fewer vehicles via dynamic trip-vehicle assignment; their contribution demonstrates fleet-level feasibility at scale, yet the reported outcome is vehicle count reduction rather than per-trip fare savings, compatibility scores, or bootstrap-validated confidence intervals at the pairwise level.

Agatz et al.~\parencite{agatz2012ridesharing} reviewed dynamic ridesharing optimisation and concluded that greedy heuristics offer a practical balance between solution quality and tractability for online matching problems. This conclusion directly supports the algorithmic choice in the present study: an $O(B^2)$ greedy matcher operating on batches of at most 20 trips within 5-minute windows is deployable in real time, whereas exact combinatorial optimisers become intractable at city scale. Furuhata et al.~\parencite{furuhata2013ridesharing} identified four matching criteria, namely temporal flexibility, spatial proximity, directional compatibility, and willingness to share, which directly inform the five-filter design of Algorithm~\ref{alg:ride_pool}. The Furuhata et al.\ framework is conceptual rather than empirical; this thesis instantiates three of the four criteria as hard filters and the fourth as an implicit assumption once filters pass, then quantifies the resulting operational trade-off.

Tang et al.~\parencite{tang2018joint} characterised the joint distribution of taxi passenger counts and waiting times, providing empirical support for the 8-minute temporal threshold used in this study. Their distributional analysis confirms that passengers tolerate only short waiting windows before abandoning a shared-ride request, validating the strict temporal filter adopted here. Zhang et al.~\parencite{zhang2023deeptrip} addressed next-trip origin prediction rather than pooling, but their finding that trip-level features alone achieve limited predictive power foreshadows the negative poolability classification result reported in \autoref{sec:pool_eval}.

\paragraph{Critical assessment for this problem.} Pooling research provides strong theoretical motivation (shareability networks show substantial consolidation potential), but operational studies rarely report pairwise savings under explicit detour and waiting constraints with uncertainty quantification. The gap between the Santi et al.\ $\sim$40\% bound and any deployable pool rate is not merely an algorithmic challenge; it reflects genuine passenger behaviour constraints that no optimiser can eliminate. For RQ3, this thesis therefore evaluates a constraint-aware greedy algorithm against both the Santi et al.\ theoretical bound and naive baselines (random pairing and temporal-only matching), reporting pool rate, compatibility score, per-trip savings, and city-scale impact with 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals. The baseline comparison in \autoref{ch:results} demonstrates that maximising pool rate alone (as random pairing does at 100\%) produces low-quality matches with minimal per-trip savings, confirming that quality-constrained matching is the appropriate optimisation target for real-world deployment.

\section{Ensemble Learning}

In the ensemble learning literature, Wolpert~\parencite{wolpert1992stacking} formalised stacked generalisation, showing that a meta-learner trained on out-of-fold predictions reduces overfitting relative to in-sample combination. Breiman~\parencite{breiman2001rf} introduced Random Forests and Friedman~\parencite{friedman2001gbm} developed Gradient Boosting Machines, establishing the foundations for the bagging and boosting strategies evaluated here. Chen and Guestrin~\parencite{chen2016xgboost} refined boosting with regularisation and sparse-matrix support to produce XGBoost, which emerges as the strongest individual predictor in the present benchmarking study. Zheng et al.~\parencite{zheng2020hybrid} demonstrated hybrid deep learning and gradient boosting for traffic speed prediction, motivating the XGBoost-LSTM hybrid in this study as a natural extension of the Kim et al.~\parencite{kim2020hybrid} LR-LSTM design.

\paragraph{Critical assessment for this problem.} Ensemble methods are well understood in general machine learning, but their application to spatio-temporal taxi demand has been piecemeal: prior studies typically report a single ensemble or a single strong baseline rather than comparing six combination strategies under identical conditions. Simple and weighted averaging provide interpretable baselines with negligible implementation cost; voting regressors offer a library-native alternative; stacking and blending represent the two dominant meta-learning paradigms (cross-validated versus holdout meta-features). The key distinction for this problem is that spatio-temporal demand exhibits \emph{regime-dependent} model strengths: linear models excel in low-demand off-peak bins while gradient-boosted trees excel during rush-hour peaks. Meta-learners that can assign negative or near-zero weights to correlated base models (as stacking does via Ridge regression) are therefore particularly well suited to this task. No prior work has systematically compared all six strategies on the same spatio-temporal demand task with non-parametric statistical validation, leaving practitioners without evidence on whether the additional complexity of stacking justifies its implementation cost relative to blending or weighted averaging.

The theoretical foundation for ensemble superiority rests on the bias-variance decomposition: combining models with partially independent errors reduces variance without increasing bias, provided the base learners make different types of mistakes~\parencite{breiman2001rf}. In spatio-temporal demand forecasting, error independence arises naturally from architectural diversity, linear models misestimate peak-hour surges that tree-based models capture, while tree-based models overfit sparse off-peak bins where linear models are adequate. Wolpert's stacked generalisation~\parencite{wolpert1992stacking} extends this principle by learning optimal combination weights rather than fixing them a priori, and the present study confirms that learned combination (Stacking, Blending) outperforms fixed-weight averaging (Simple, Weighted, Voting) on all five metrics.

Hybrid deep-boosting architectures such as the XGBoost-LSTM variant evaluated here represent a middle ground between single-model and ensemble approaches~\parencite{zheng2020hybrid}. By replacing the linear first stage of the Kim et al.~\parencite{kim2020hybrid} LR-LSTM with a gradient-boosted tree, the hybrid captures non-linear trend structure before LSTM residual modelling. However, the present results show that even this stronger hybrid trails Stacking and Blending on RMSE, suggesting that the greatest gains come from combining diverse model families at the meta-learner level rather than refining the internal architecture of any single hybrid pipeline. This finding has practical implications: practitioners should invest engineering effort in ensemble infrastructure before pursuing architectural novelty in individual models.

The relationship between ensemble complexity and deployment cost deserves explicit attention. Simple Averaging requires training three base models and computing a weighted sum at inference time; Stacking requires training seven base models five times each (for out-of-fold predictions) plus a meta-learner, increasing training time by an order of magnitude. The present results show that this additional complexity yields a 4.4\% RMSE reduction over the best individual model (XGBoost), and a 9.0\% reduction over Simple Averaging. Whether this improvement justifies the deployment cost depends on the operator's accuracy requirements and computational budget; the multi-metric comparison in \autoref{ch:results} provides the evidence base for such decisions.

\section{Positioning of This Thesis}

The three literature threads reviewed above converge on a coherent set of unmet needs that this thesis is designed to address. In demand forecasting, the field has progressed from ARIMA baselines through deep spatio-temporal networks to hybrid residual architectures, yet comparative studies either restrict evaluation to a single model family or report accuracy without establishing statistical significance. In ride pooling, theoretical shareability bounds and fleet-level assignment frameworks demonstrate that consolidation is feasible at city scale, but operational algorithms evaluated under realistic passenger constraints with pairwise economic quantification remain scarce. In ensemble learning, stacking, blending, and averaging strategies are well established in general machine learning, but no prior study has benchmarked six combination approaches on identical spatio-temporal taxi demand features with accompanying Friedman and Wilcoxon testing.

This thesis positions itself at the intersection of these three gaps. It adopts the Kim et al.~\parencite{kim2020hybrid} LR-LSTM benchmark and the Santi et al.~\parencite{santi2014quantifying} shareability bound as explicit reference points, extending the former through a fourteen-model ensemble study with non-parametric validation (RQ1, RQ2), and the latter through a five-filter greedy matcher with bootstrap confidence intervals and city-scale impact projection (RQ3). The Furuhata et al.~\parencite{furuhata2013ridesharing} matching criteria and the Agatz et al.~\parencite{agatz2012ridesharing} tractability analysis inform the algorithmic design choices, while the Wolpert~\parencite{wolpert1992stacking} stacking framework and Chen and Guestrin~\parencite{chen2016xgboost} XGBoost implementation provide the ensemble building blocks. Poongodi et al.~\parencite{poongodi2022nyc} and Liu et al.~\parencite{liu2021epidemic} demonstrate that XGBoost-family models are strong on NYC taxi data, motivating their inclusion as both individual predictors and ensemble base learners in the present framework.

By evaluating forecasting and pooling within a single reproducible pipeline on the same NYC Yellow Taxi dataset, the thesis enables practitioners to assess both predictive accuracy and operational consolidation returns under unified assumptions, a combination absent from the prior work summarised in \autoref{tab:litreview}. The chronological 11-month train-test gap, the $K{=}40$ cluster granularity validated by inertia and silhouette criteria, and the five-metric evaluation suite with Friedman testing collectively ensure that reported results are conservative, reproducible, and directly comparable across the fourteen forecasting models and the pooling algorithm evaluated here.

The positioning relative to each literature thread can be summarised as follows. Against demand forecasting studies, this thesis extends the Kim et al.~\parencite{kim2020hybrid} hybrid benchmark from a single-neighbourhood evaluation to a citywide 40-cluster study with fourteen models and statistical validation, while maintaining the same prediction target (cluster-bin pickup counts) for direct comparability. Against pooling studies, the thesis operationalises the Furuhata et al.~\parencite{furuhata2013ridesharing} matching criteria as a deployable greedy algorithm and quantifies returns under constraints that the Santi et al.~\parencite{santi2014quantifying} shareability bound does not enforce. Against ensemble studies, the thesis provides the first controlled comparison of six combination strategies on identical spatio-temporal features with Friedman testing, filling the gap left by studies that report a single ensemble without cross-strategy benchmarking.

The choice of NYC Yellow Taxi data as the common experimental substrate is deliberate: it is the largest publicly available urban taxi corpus, it supports direct comparison with multiple prior benchmarks~\parencite{kim2020hybrid,xu2017realtime,liu2021epidemic,santi2014quantifying}, and its rich spatial and temporal attributes enable both cluster-level forecasting and trip-level pooling evaluation. Alternative datasets such as Chicago taxi records or ride-hailing platform data were considered but rejected because they lack the combination of public availability, prior benchmark coverage, and trip-level GPS coordinates required for the pooling algorithm.

\section{Synthesis of Gaps}

Integrating the three literature threads reviewed above reveals a coherent pattern of unmet needs that no single prior study addresses comprehensively.

In demand forecasting, the field has progressed from ARIMA baselines through deep spatio-temporal networks to hybrid residual architectures, yet three gaps persist: (i)~controlled comparison of diverse model families on identical targets and features; (ii)~non-parametric significance testing to establish whether reported accuracy differences are robust; and (iii)~evaluation under chronological splits that test genuine out-of-sample generalisation rather than interpolation within a continuous time series. Studies such as~\parencite{kim2020hybrid} and~\parencite{xu2017realtime} report strong individual-model performance but do not establish statistical significance across alternatives; studies such as~\parencite{liu2021epidemic} achieve high $R^2$ on overlapping data but during a structurally disrupted period (COVID-19) that limits generalisability to pre-pandemic operational regimes.

In ride pooling, theoretical shareability bounds~\parencite{santi2014quantifying} and fleet-level assignment frameworks~\parencite{alonso2017demand} demonstrate consolidation feasibility at city scale, but operational studies rarely report pairwise savings under explicit detour and waiting constraints with uncertainty quantification. The gap between the $\sim$40\% theoretical bound and any deployable pool rate is not merely algorithmic; it reflects genuine passenger behaviour constraints that no optimiser can eliminate. Without bootstrap confidence intervals and baseline comparisons, operators cannot assess whether a reported pool rate represents genuine operational value or an artefact of permissive matching criteria.

In ensemble learning, stacking, blending, and averaging strategies are well established in general machine learning~\parencite{wolpert1992stacking,breiman2001rf,chen2016xgboost}, but their application to spatio-temporal taxi demand has been piecemeal. No prior study compares six combination strategies on identical features with accompanying non-parametric validation, leaving practitioners without evidence on whether the additional complexity of stacking justifies its implementation cost relative to simpler alternatives.

The intersection of these three gaps defines the contribution space of the present thesis: a unified pipeline that benchmarks fourteen forecasting models with statistical validation, evaluates constraint-aware pooling with bootstrap confidence intervals and city-scale impact projection, and connects both subsystems through a common data regime and reproducibility standard. \autoref{tab:litreview} makes this intersection visible: only the present work marks both the ``Stat.\ Val.'' and ``Pool'' columns affirmatively.

\section{Summary}

Across demand forecasting, ride pooling, and ensemble learning, three recurring gaps emerge. Forecasting studies rarely apply non-parametric significance testing across model classes. Pooling studies often report theoretical upper bounds rather than operational algorithms under passenger-acceptance constraints. Few works jointly quantify trip-level and city-scale economic and environmental impact within the same experimental framework. This thesis addresses these gaps through the three research questions introduced in \autoref{ch:introduction}, evaluated within a single reproducible pipeline on two years of NYC Yellow Taxi records.
